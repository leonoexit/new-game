# Prototype 2 — One Spring, One Life hands-on checklist

Complete two free-form 14-day Springs. Do not declare a goal before either run: let a different priority emerge through play. Reload at least once during each run.

The question is whether a finite Spring creates a meaningful sacrifice and an ending specific enough to suggest another way of living.

## Before the runs

- [ ] Confirm day 1 opens directly with no objective or Goal selection.
- [ ] Confirm a Weather card is visible on the table and shows today's Sun or Rain.
- [ ] Tap Weather and confirm the condition, seed and full 14-day forecast are readable.
- [ ] Confirm reloading does not change the condition or forecast.
- [ ] Confirm the Prototype 1 farm was not imported or overwritten.
- [ ] Confirm Hand, tap, drag and the ordinary card grammar still feel intact.

## Person interaction feedback

- [ ] Spend Time with a present Person and confirm a short speech bubble appears beside that Person.
- [ ] Share Produce and confirm the bubble acknowledges the Share rather than looking like a global system message.
- [ ] Confirm each successful moment still costs exactly 1 AP; Share also consumes exactly one Produce.
- [ ] Confirm a second same-day moment and a repeated signature reject without losing AP or Produce.
- [ ] Confirm the bubble disappears by itself and does not become a dialogue tree.
- [ ] Confirm relationship changes still come only from distinct contextual Memory.

## Run A — let the farm pull you

This label is only a testing contrast, not an in-game objective.

- [ ] Play freely, but notice whether crop care, Quality, harvest timing or shipping becomes your main concern.
- [ ] Allow at least one Person opportunity to compete with farm work.
- [ ] Reload once and confirm seed, forecast, farm state, Memory and AP telemetry survive.
- [ ] End day 7 and confirm the Week Journal opens.
- [ ] If the Journal contains many memories, confirm its middle scrolls while `Begin Week 2` and `New Spring` remain reachable.
- [ ] Continue to day 8 and confirm Person schedules repeat during week 2.
- [ ] End day 14 and confirm growth and shipping resolve once before the Chronicle.
- [ ] Confirm the run cannot advance to day 15.

## Run B — let people pull you

Again, this is a test contrast chosen during play, not a setup Goal.

- [ ] Begin a New Spring and confirm the world and Memory reset under a new seed.
- [ ] Play freely, but notice whether repeated Person moments become your main concern.
- [ ] Do enough farming that Person time competes with watering, travel, harvest or crop care.
- [ ] Reload once and confirm seed, forecast, relationships, telemetry and world state survive.
- [ ] Finish day 14 and confirm the Chronicle remains scrollable and its action buttons remain reachable.

## Chronicle review after each run

- [ ] Every sentence can be traced to visible farm state, Memory or actions actually taken.
- [ ] The Chronicle describes the farm, remembered people and broad AP emphasis.
- [ ] It interprets `Care for the Land`, `Know Your Neighbors` or no single dominant path from play; it does not claim you selected one.
- [ ] It does not describe another path as failed or unfinished.
- [ ] There is no score, star rating or efficiency grade.
- [ ] `Review farm` returns to the final board and `Chronicle` opens the ending again.
- [ ] `Begin a new Spring` creates a clean run with a new seed and no setup choice.

## Report back

For each run, record:

1. What priority emerged while you were playing?
2. What concrete opportunity did you knowingly give up?
3. Did Person time meaningfully compete with farming?
4. Did Weather feel like a real card in the world or merely a forecast button?
5. Did the Person bubble make the interaction clear and feel attached to that person?
6. Which Chronicle sentence felt most specific, and which felt generic or wrong?
7. Did the ending suggest a different next run?
8. Did 14 days feel too short, sufficient or repetitive?

Prototype 2 is validated only if the two runs create recognizably different priorities and at least one sacrifice matters. Passing automated tests means only that the build is ready for this checklist.
